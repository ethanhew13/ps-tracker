Grand Theft Auto VI

NZ release: 19 November 2026

Graphic violence, drug use, offensive language, sex scenes, and nudity Rated on: 02 October 2026

Grand Theft Auto 6 | GTA 6 | GTA VI

What’s it about?

The highly anticipated sixth instalment of this game is set in the fictional state of sunny Leonida, based on Florida. The game follows two playable protagonists, Jason and Lucia, who have always known the deck is stacked against them. Players follow their story and relationship after an easy score goes wrong, pulling them into a criminal conspiracy where they must fight to stay alive.

The facts

  • Developed, published, and distributed by Rockstar Games
  • English language
  • Console game released for different console platforms as well as online gaming
  • Almost every edition of GTA has been classified R18 in New Zealand due to the adult nature of the game play

Why did it get this rating?

This video game was classified by Te Mana Whakaatu, Classification Office. You can find out more about classification process and ratings here.

Graphic violence

The urban, real-world, civilian context of the game heightens the impact of its violence. Because the game is open-world, players have the choice to try to escape or fight. They may attack and kill at will, with nearby police, gangsters, and civilians responding with force.

Players can use fists, bladed and blunt weapons, firearms, explosives, and Molotov cocktails.

Injuries cause significant blood splatter, with blood covering surfaces and staining clothes. Bullets cause wounds, and blood pools from bodies. Powerful guns can cause bloody decapitation and dismemberment, causing ragged flesh and bones to be shown. Similar gory depictions of dismemberment are caused by injury from heavy vehicles.

Players can continue to shoot and dismember victims once they are dead, though there is no reason to do so.

Victims clutch at their wounds in pain and react believably to impacts. Those who are burnt by Molotov cocktails briefly flail and scream before dying.

This violence is able to be inflicted on both "hostiles" and civilians, so the effect is strongly violent and cruel.

Drug use

Drug trafficking, drug manufacture, and drug use are portrayed in the game. Some specifics include:

  • While visiting criminal locations, players will encounter scenes depicting drug manufacture and distribution, showing people grinding, treating and packing white powder that is assumed to be cocaine.
  • Throughout the story, Lucia and Jason acquire drug parcels to sell for money.
  • In the game, cannabis can be used. Players can buy joints or use from a shared pipe. This causes game play footage and colours to blur. While this somewhat normalises drug use, it is presented in an American context where the drug is legal.
  • Cocaine use is shown in different ways. A comically manic character snorts and spills cocaine. In one scene, players may choose for Jason to take cocaine in a chaotic party montage. By making this choice it negatively affects his relationship with Lucia, which is indicated by an icon

Sex scenes

There is strong sexual content in the game, ranging from tender, loving scenes to BDSM. Examples include:

  • There are soft sex scenes between Jason and Lucia as their relationship progresses. These focus on expression and caressing, with some brief breast nudity.
  • At a party, there are stronger sex scenes in various rooms. This includes full female nudity with obvious sexual motion and vocalisations, though shown from a distance. One scene is BDSM-themed, showing a sex toy being used on a man in a gimp suit.
  • Players are able to pay for private shows at a strip club. The dancers gyrate, bare-breasted, and say teasing lines. Players can move characters to touch the dancers' breasts.

Nudity

In nudist areas, particularly beaches, there is some incidental non-sexual nudity. This includes breast, buttock and full-frontal genital nudity.

Offensive language

There is regular use of the word "f*ck" in emphasis and exclamation, and occasionally used in aggression to elevate a tense situation. Other terms include "sh*t", "c*nt" and "bitch". Offensive language occurs contextually in responses during gameplay, which varies depending on the play.

The N-word slur is also used colloquially, which may offend some people.

Suicide

In one mission, an incidental character may step off a building rather than be questioned. The body and blood pooling are examined afterwards. Though the death is sudden, there is little sense of distress and the impact is limited.

When content stays with you

We all have our boundaries, and it’s completely okay if something you’ve watched is weighing on your mind. If certain content lingers with you, consider having a chat with friends or whānau to debrief about what you’ve just seen. But if you’re still feeling affected, please reach out to any of the following helplines for support.

  • 1737
  • Safe to talk
  • Lifeline
  • Youthline
